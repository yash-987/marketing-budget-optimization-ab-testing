# A/B Testing Analysis — Marketing Optimization

## 1. Objective

Evaluate whether users exposed to actual advertisements had a higher conversion rate than users shown public service announcements (PSAs).

## 2. Experiment Design

- **Treatment group:** Ad
- **Control group:** PSA
- **Outcome metric:** Conversion rate
- **Null hypothesis (H₀):** The conversion rates of the two groups are equal.
- **Alternative hypothesis (H₁):** The conversion rates of the two groups differ.
- **Significance level:** 5%
- **Statistical test:** Two-proportion z-test

## 3. Experiment Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Ad group conversion rate | 2.5547% |
| PSA group conversion rate | 1.7854% |
| Absolute uplift | +0.7692 percentage points |
| Relative uplift | +43.09% |
| Z-statistic | 7.3701 |
| P-value | 1.7053 × 10⁻¹³ |
| 95% confidence interval for absolute uplift | +0.5873 to +0.936 percentage points |

## 4. Business Interpretation

The advertising treatment group demonstrated a higher conversion rate than the PSA control group. The difference was statistically significant at the 5% significance level.

At the observed conversion rates, a comparison of 10,000 users per group corresponds to approximately 77 additional conversions in the treatment group.

This is a rate-based estimate and not a guarantee of future results.

## 5. Business Recommendation

The results support the effectiveness of the advertising treatment in improving conversion rate. However, a final profitability or rollout decision requires additional information about advertising costs, revenue per conversion, and profit margins.

The A/B testing dataset does not independently establish incremental revenue, customer acquisition cost, or return on advertising spend.

## 6. Limitations

- The analysis measures conversion effectiveness, not profitability.
- Statistical significance does not guarantee commercial significance.
- Daily and hourly analyses are descriptive and do not independently establish causal differences between time periods.
- Business rollout decisions should consider costs, expected returns, and the reliability of the experiment design.

## 7. Conclusion

The advertising treatment produced a statistically significant increase in conversion rate compared with the PSA control group. Further financial analysis is required before recommending a full commercial rollout.
